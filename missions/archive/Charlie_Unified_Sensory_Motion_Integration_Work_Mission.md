### 1A. Sensory source contract

Each installed sensor has exactly one authoritative lifecycle/acquisition owner per physical resource. That owner opens, configures, polls/captures, recovers, timestamps, publishes health/fault/status and shuts down the source. Publishing occurs independently of consumer interest, at a safe bounded rate when authorized. Expensive or invasive acquisition may be gated without losing discoverability. Shared streams offer independent bounded subscriptions plus `latest`/`recent` queries with freshness and explicit fault invalidation. No consumer can silently create a second hardware owner. Cross-process and cross-body ownership require real leases, boot identity, clock-domain mapping and truthful freshness limitations, not unverified assumptions.

Create or extend **a modality-capable source directory/registry** using the existing acquisition, `StimulusBus`, `CameraFeeder`, `PassivePublisher` and related interfaces; generalize only as necessary. Every source record describes stable ID, body/device, modality and schema; authoritative owner; route/endpoint; availability/health; actual rate; capture/receive clock with offset uncertainty; retention bounds and evictions; provenance; access/safety/privacy requirements; and explicit unknowns. Camera pixels, scalar range, yaw/pose, IMU, thermal/health, bumper/line sensors (if truly installed), and remote camera readings need compatible publication semantics, not necessarily identical payload formats. Avoid forcing scalar telemetry into a fake camera frame or broadcasting full-rate raw bytes through a registry. Consumers request what is available, track source identity and reject stale, unavailable, out-of-order or incompatible measurements. Historical, synthetic and actual observations remain distinguishable.

## 2. Qualified actuation: one owner per motor group

Actuation is the **dual of sensing**, not simply another sensor and not a direct subscriber callback. Use the existing capability/resource directory for discoverability/readiness and share **actuator state/outcome telemetry** as sensory-like observations. Provide a small reusable command/request contract that all authorized behavior clients use. Do not create an independent goal chooser or generic motor framework that replaces qualified device-specific controls.

### 2A. Intent/request vs motion execution

The Learning Executive owns goals and developmental interests; existing AttentionManager owns attention, PPAL's chooser/planner owns tactical action proposals. Potential future intention/planning services use the **same resource request boundary**, not privileged direct hardware calls. A request identifies source/owner, purpose, body and actuator, target/pose/velocity where meaningful, bounded duration and rate, priority/preemption semantics, freshness/source-frame dependencies, required qualification and safety evidence, cancel/expiry, software/firmware/calibration identity, and a correlation ID. An acceptance receipt is not proof of physical consequence.

One authority-bound actuator executor owns actual motor commands and arbitration *for each motor group*: admission, exclusive leases, resource conflict/preemption, calibration envelopes, environmental/readiness gates, command serialization, finite hardware-local expiries, watchdog, STOP/retreat, cancellation, fault latching, startup/restart policy and telemetry. Duplicate/stale/replayed/out-of-order requests must be refused. Priority inversion and STOP latency have explicit bounds. Stop, emergency stop and physical cutoffs must not be blocked by ordinary requests, camera inference or slow subscribers. When STOP delivery is uncertain, fail closed against another start and report physical outcome as unknown. Critical low-level interlocks must remain on device/firmware where feasible; a Python timeout, network heartbeat or process callback alone is not independent failsafe protection. Authority and qualification are never inferred from catalog presence, Executive selection, an ACK, a synthetic fixture or a confidence score.

Do not confuse publisher/subscriber availability with unrestricted remote actuation: state/telemetry may be broadly observable; commands require authenticated/authorized guarded admission and an exclusive owner. Describe which interlocks live on MCU/RP2040 and which on Pi. Never let a remote body assume the Pi's monotonic clock matches its own.

### 2C. RP2040 servos and CAL-1

A head request is not a command for the caller to open serial or bypass the qualified firmware.
